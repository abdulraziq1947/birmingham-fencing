Fence Installation Birmingham

Home/Fence Installation Birmingham

Fence installation covers a new boundary where none exists, or full replacement of an existing run. Work includes setting out post centres, digging footings into clay or Mercia mudstone subsoil, concreting posts, fitting rails and panels, and attaching gravel boards where the specification requires them.

Installation process

Posts are set in concrete with depth and diameter suited to fence height and soil type. On sloping gardens — common in suburbs such as Moseley, Kings Heath and Selly Oak on the clay-heavy suburbs — panels are stepped or raked to follow ground level while keeping a consistent top line.

Old fencing removal and disposal are listed separately in the quote when required.

Residential and commercial installation

Residential work includes garden fencing and gates. Commercial and security installations use metal systems — see commercial fencing and security fencing.

Frequently asked questions

How long does installation take?expand_more

Many residential runs complete within one to three days depending on length, ground conditions and removal of old fencing. Duration is stated in the quote.

Can installation proceed in wet weather?expand_more

Light rain does not usually stop post setting. Heavy rain or frozen ground can delay concrete cure — we reschedule if conditions would affect footing quality.

What is included in an installation quote?expand_more

Post setting, rails, panels or boards, gravel boards where specified, and gate hanging if part of the scope. Removal of old fencing is listed separately when required.

Do you install on sloping gardens?expand_more

Yes. Panels are stepped or raked to follow ground level while keeping a consistent top line — common on sloped plots in suburbs such as Moseley, Harborne and Kings Heath.

How does Birmingham soil affect post footings?expand_more

Clay and Mercia mudstone soils hold moisture and can be dense to dig. Footing depth and post diameter are sized to fence height and local ground conditions, which we assess when measuring.
